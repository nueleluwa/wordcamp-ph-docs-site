# Code of Conduct & Safety

<Callout type="warning">
  **Status: draft baseline, not yet formally ratified.** The source planning materials establish that WCPHC must be "open to all participants regardless of experience level" ([policies/wordcamp-central-compliance.md](wordcamp-central-compliance)) but do not name who enforces conduct standards or what the reporting process is. This page proposes a standard, WordCamp-appropriate baseline so the team is operating from something rather than nothing. It should be reviewed and formally adopted by the Lead Organizer, and this notice removed once that happens. See [organisation/open-decisions-and-gaps.md](../organisation/open-decisions-and-gaps).
</Callout>

## Why this needs to exist regardless of source material

An "open to all" event with 500–1,000+ attendees, volunteers, speakers, and sponsors needs a working answer to "what happens if someone is harassed or made unsafe" before it needs the answer, not after. Attendee, speaker, and volunteer application/registration flows referenced throughout this documentation (see [recruiting/](../recruiting/), [marketing-and-communications/attendee-communications.md](../marketing-and-communications/attendee-communications)) should link to whatever COC is formally adopted.

## Proposed baseline expectations

- WCPHC is dedicated to providing a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, age, or religion.
- Harassment includes offensive comments, deliberate intimidation, stalking, unwelcome photography or recording, sustained disruption of talks or events, inappropriate physical contact, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Organizers, volunteers, speakers, sponsors, and attendees are all expected to follow the same standard. There's no exemption for seniority, sponsorship tier, or speaker status.

## Proposed reporting process

<Callout type="error">
  Report immediately to any organizer. This bypasses the general complaint-handling process and escalates straight to the Lead Organizer. See [event-day/issue-response-playbook.md](../event-day/issue-response-playbook).
</Callout>

1. Anyone experiencing or witnessing a violation reports it immediately to any organizer, who escalates directly to the Lead Organizer. Per [event-day/issue-response-playbook.md](../event-day/issue-response-playbook), this bypasses the general complaint-handling row and goes straight to the top.
2. The Lead Organizer (or a delegate agreed in advance) investigates and decides on a response, which may include a warning, removal from the event without refund, or a ban from future events.
3. The reporter's confidentiality is protected to the extent possible.
4. Every report is documented, regardless of outcome, in the post-event internal review. See [post-event/post-event-workflow.md](../post-event/post-event-workflow).
