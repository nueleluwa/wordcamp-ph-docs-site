## What the report is for

The weekly report is the mechanism that lets the Lead Organizer see the health of every team without attending every team's meeting. It surfaces three things that a dashboard alone can't:

1. **Narrative context** — why a number moved, not just that it moved
2. **Blockers** — problems a team can't solve on its own
3. **Escalations** — decisions or support that need to come from outside the team

## Structure

The template covers: header info (week, reporter, team), executive summary (wins, challenges, items needing escalation), metrics update (last week / this week / change for each of the team's key KPIs), this week's activities (completed / in progress), next week's plan (top 3 priorities, key meetings), and blockers & support needed.

## How reports are used

- **Blockers** feed directly into the weekly organizer meeting agenda — see [planning/weekly-operating-rhythm.md](../planning/weekly-operating-rhythm).
- **Items needing escalation** that involve a decision nobody present can make alone get logged in [organisation/open-decisions-and-gaps.md](../organisation/open-decisions-and-gaps).
- **Metrics** should match what's on the dashboard exactly — the report explains the number, it doesn't re-derive it. See [reporting-and-kpis/dashboard-and-tracking-setup.md](dashboard-and-tracking-setup).
- Reports are archived, not discarded — they're a primary input to the post-event internal review ("what worked well / what needs improvement") in [post-event/post-event-workflow.md](../post-event/post-event-workflow).

## What makes a good report vs. a bad one

A bad report restates dashboard numbers with no context ("Registrations: 45"). A good report explains what moved the number and what to do next ("Registrations: 45 (+12 this week) — jump came from the UNIPORT campus visit; next week we're repeating the same visit format at RSU"). Reports exist to transfer judgment, not just data — the dashboard already has the data.

## If a team has nothing to report

Submit the report anyway with "no change" explicitly stated. A missing report is indistinguishable from a team that forgot to report versus a team with nothing going on — don't make the Lead Organizer guess which one happened.
